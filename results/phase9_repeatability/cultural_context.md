# Context and assumptions

These are researcher-chosen function-preserving recontextualizations, not identity claims about people or proof that an object is unique to a country. Product photographs establish visible objects; culture labels rely on metadata, contextual references and explicit assumptions. No national origin is inferred from pixels alone.

- Case1 French pastry to Indian snack: inherited user-accepted baseline. The samosa reference is documented as an Indian roadside food photograph; it is not exclusive to India.
- Case2 German-associated pretzel to French-associated croissant: both single bakery snacks. Germany's official tourism recipe documents Bavarian pretzels: https://www.germany.travel/media/redaktion/erleben_geniessen_content/deutsche_rezepte/rezept_pdf/en_14/GermanBreadCulture_Bavaria.pdf . French association is the inherited project objective; the actual croissant photograph is by Petr Kratochvil, with no claim about its shooting location.
- Case3 American-style chocolate-chip cookie to Japanese grilled rice snack: both portable snacks, while sweetness/ingredients differ. The target file explicitly depicts yaki-onigiri, by Ocdp; Japan National Tourism Organization describes onigiri as a snack: https://www.japan.travel/en/my/muslim-guide/travel-ideas/conbini-a-one-stop-shop-for-food-and-amenities/ . We do not claim identical nutrition, recipe or meal conventions.
- Cases4/5 Italian Borsalino fedora and Moroccan-associated fez: both headwear on an isolated display. The fedora photo is explicitly a Borsalino hat; the brand/museum documents its Alessandria origins: https://borsalinomuseum.com/en/the-museum/ . Commons describes the fez's Moroccan naming and wider regional use: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Fez_(hat) . The photographed fez is not certified to be made/worn in Morocco; no national exclusivity or present-day universal dress convention is asserted.
- Case6 generic handled mug to Japanese-style wabi-sabi tea vessel: both drinking vessels. Japan tourism documents chawan in tea practice: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/tea-ceremony/ . The selected reference is explicitly a contemporary wabi-sabi tea bowl, photographed by ottmarliebert.com; we do not claim it was manufactured in Japan or is approved for a specific ceremony. The original mug is culturally widespread, so this is the pilot's weakest source-culture specificity.

All five new cultural judgments remain for actual user review. A recognizable licensed cutout is not a culturally evaluated generative transformation.
